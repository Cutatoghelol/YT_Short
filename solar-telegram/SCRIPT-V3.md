# The Sun Sent a Telegram — V3

The [spoken script](src/v3/dialogue.json) includes dramatized dialogue and reactions. Telegraph operators in Boston and Portland reported exchanging messages on September 2, 1859, with their batteries disconnected. The burning paper is a separate reenactment of an electrical incident reported during the storm. [Prescott's account](https://ajsonline.org/api/v1/articles/66165-the-great-auroral-exhibition-of-august-28th-to-september-4th-1859.pdf) · [Contemporary accounts compiled by NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050210157/downloads/20050210157.pdf) · [Loomis's collected reports, pp. 322–323](https://ajsonline.org/article/64677.pdf)

The terrain cutaway depicts how rapid changes in Earth's magnetic field can induce a geoelectric field at the surface and drive current through grounded long lines. The aurora is another effect of the storm. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/geomagnetism/science/geomagnetically-induced-currents)
