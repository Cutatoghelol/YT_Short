# The Sun Sent a Telegram

On September 2, 1859, during the storm now known as the Carrington Event, the Boston and Portland operators reported communicating after taking both batteries off the line. The office interiors and gestures are dramatized. The sounder reproduces the timing of an exchange rather than a transcription of the actual messages. [Contemporary newspaper accounts collected by NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20050210157/downloads/20050210157.pdf) · [Historical analysis by D. H. Boteler](https://ostrnrcan-dostrncan.canada.ca/handle/1845/160072)

Changes in Earth's magnetic field induce a geoelectric field, which can drive current through grounded long-distance lines. A magnet and wire coil illustrate induction on a smaller scale. The aurora and the induced current are effects of the same geomagnetic disturbance. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/geomagnetism/science/geomagnetically-induced-currents)

## Narration

### 0–4

They disconnected the batteries. The telegraph kept clicking.

### 4–14

In eighteen fifty-nine, a wire ran between Boston and Portland, Maine. An operator tapped a key in one office. The battery sent pulses down the line, and a metal arm in the other office clicked out the message.

### 14–26

Then the line started acting up. The current would surge, then fade. The Boston operator asked Portland to take its battery off the line. Both batteries were disconnected. The line should have gone quiet. But Portland could still read Boston's signal.

### 26–44

The disturbance started at the Sun. A solar storm shook Earth's magnetic field. Think of a magnet and a coil of wire. Hold the magnet still, and the needle stays put. Move it, and the needle jumps. Changes in Earth's magnetic field created an electric field that pushed current through long, grounded wires. The same storm lit up the sky with auroras.

### 44–56

Back in the offices, the operators still had to tap their keys to send each message. For a while, the current from the storm was strong enough to work the line, even with both batteries disconnected. They went back to sending messages.

### 56–62

They switched off the batteries. Nobody could switch off the Sun.
